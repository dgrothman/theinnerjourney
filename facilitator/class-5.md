# Class 5 · Self Esteem & the Drama Triangle — Teaching Notes

**Teaching hour:** 60 min · **Group hour:** follows
**Big idea:** Healthy self-esteem is the inner experience of our own preciousness and value. When we don't have it, we end up on the Drama Triangle; we get off by owning our reality and moving toward the Empowerment Dynamic.
**Bring:** Class 5 handout (exercise + take-home), pens.
**Builds on:** Class 4 (Self Care). Reality, Boundaries, Self Care and Moderation are the tools that grow self-esteem.

> **⚑ Review** marks anything added beyond the slides, the site articles and the old teaching notes.

## Head: What & Why (12 min)

### Slide 4 — Healthy self-esteem
- Definition, word for word: *"Healthy self-esteem is the internal experience of one's own preciousness and value as a person."* (slide)
- It comes from inside a person and moves outward into relationships. (*Difficulty Experiencing Appropriate Levels of Self-Esteem*)
- Healthy people know they are valuable and precious even when they make a mistake, face an angry person, are cheated or lied to, or are rejected by a lover, friend, parent, child or boss. They may still feel guilt, fear, anger and pain, but the sense of worth stays intact. (*Difficulty Experiencing Appropriate Levels of Self-Esteem*)
- Say: "Feelings come and go. Value stays."

### Slide 5 — What is the issue?
- Codependents have difficulty with self-esteem at one or both extremes.
- Low self-esteem: "I think I am worth less than others. I don't measure up."
  - Comes from caregivers giving, in words or without them, the message that we are "less-than." Those messages become our own opinion of ourselves. (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)
  - As adults it is almost impossible to generate the feeling of value from within. (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)
- Arrogant: "I think I am superior to others. I have learned to find faults in others, making them inferior to me."
  - Two roots. The family taught us to find fault in others, so we rise above shame by judging. Or the family taught us we are superior and can do no wrong, a false sense of power. (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)
  - A false sense of superiority sabotages relationships as much as "less-than" does. (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)
- Both result in "Other-Esteem": we get our value from external things, from our "human doing" or from other people's opinions and behavior.
  - Examples: performance, appearance, wealth, friends, cars, jobs, our children's performance, a spouse's power, degrees. (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)
  - Say: *"Other-esteem is fragile. It comes from outside us, so we can lose it at any time."* (*Where Self-Esteem Issues Come From*)

### Slide 6 — Healthy self-esteem
- Three columns; each links back to a core symptom:
  - Value (Self-Esteem, Reality): I am enough. I matter. I am perfectly imperfect.
  - Power (Boundaries, Reality, Moderation): I have enough control. I can influence my environment. I am in control of myself.
  - Abundance (Self-Care): I have enough. I can survive. I have joy in my life.
- Tie it back: this is what the earlier classes were building toward.
- ⚑ Review: ask the room to pick one line from the table that feels hardest to say about themselves. No sharing needed.

### Slide 7 — Unhealthy self-esteem
- The same three columns, flipped:
  - Value: Worthless. Prestige, other-esteem, "I am never enough."
  - Power: Helpless. Power over others, negative control, "I never have enough control."
  - Abundance: Hopeless. Money and possessions, hoarding, "I never have enough."
- From the site table (*Healthy*): when the outside source stops, your value drops. Power over others drops when your value drops. Money and possessions drop when value and power drop.
- Connect to Class 3: negative control is crossing someone's internal boundary.
- ⚑ Review: note the pattern. The unhealthy side is always something outside us that we chase or grip, which is why it never feels like enough.

## Hand: Motivation (16 min)

### Slide 8 — Drama Triangle (source)
- Section divider. Content is from *The Three Faces of Victim* by Lynne Forrest.
- Say: "When our worth is shaky, we reach for roles to feel safe. Here are the roles."

### Slide 9 — What is the Drama Triangle?
- Anytime we refuse to take responsibility for ourselves, we are playing victim.
- Three roles: Persecutor, Rescuer, Victim. All three are faces of victim; every role ends back at victim.
- It is a "shame machine." Moving around the triangle keeps the shame messages going.
- One-up, one-down: Rescuer and Persecutor come across as "better than." The Victim is one-down, and resentment builds until they retaliate.
- Tie back to Class 3: resentment is always a boundary issue.

### Slide 10 — The triangle
- Diagram: Rescuer, Persecutor, Victim.
- Say: *"I call it a 'shame machine' because through it we unconsciously re-enact our vicious cycles, thereby creating shame."*
- ⚑ Review: draw the triangle on a whiteboard or point to the slide, with Victim at the bottom point. Trace an arrow around it as you read the next slide.

### Slide 11 — Round and round the triangle
- Starting gate position: our most familiar role, learned in our family.
- We still rotate through all three, many times a day.
- Example from the slide: Mom threatens Junior (persecutor); Dad defends him (rescuer). Mom turns on Dad (victim), or Junior does: "I don't need your help!"
- Inside our heads: "I'm so lazy" (persecutor to victim). "I would have finished if it weren't for..." (rescuer).
- Ask: "Which role is your starting gate?" Rhetorical for now; it comes back in the exercise.

### Slide 12 — The Rescuer
- The enabler, fixer and mediator. A twisted version of the desire to nurture and protect.
- Taking care of others is how they connect and feel worth.
- Core belief: my needs don't count. "If I take care of others well enough, long enough, I'll get my turn."
- Greatest fear: nobody will be there for me. Makes themselves indispensable and fosters dependency.
- Around the triangle: as victim, martyrdom ("After all I've done for you..."). As persecutor, withdraws their care-taking.
- Connect to Class 3: this is enabling and enmeshment.

### Slide 13 — The Persecutor
- The blamer. A twisted version of the desire to protect and provide.
- Shame-based anger covers feelings of worthlessness. Must be right: preaching, blaming, lecturing, attacking, getting even.
- Core belief: the world is dangerous. "Get them before they get me!"
- Greatest fear: powerlessness. Denies their weaknesses; needs someone to blame.
- As victim: "If it weren't for you, I wouldn't have had to..."
- Connect to slide 5: this is the arrogant extreme of self-esteem in action.

### Slide 14 — The Victim
- "I can't make it." A twisted version of the innocent, vulnerable child within.
- Denies their problem-solving ability and their own power.
- Core belief and greatest fear: I can't make it on my own. Always looking for someone more capable to carry them.
- Rescues from one-down: "You're the only one who can help me."
- As persecutor: sabotages help, plays "Yes, but...", uses guilt to hook a Rescuer.
- Connect to slide 5: this is the low self-esteem extreme in action.

### Slide 15 — How we end up on the triangle
- Denied feelings: keeping others from feeling bad, or not allowing our own feelings.
- Dishonesty: when we deny our feelings we can't know (or tell) our truth.
- Failed intimacy: believing we are unlovable makes keeping our distance feel necessary.
- Result: no one takes responsibility, and everyone ends up hurt and angry.
- Say: "Hold onto these three. One of the group questions asks which one is yours."

## Heart: Tools (22 min)

### Slide 16 — How do I get off the drama triangle?
- Become aware of my starting gate position.
- Take responsibility for myself (own my reality).
- Allow myself my feelings; allow others theirs without rescuing.
- Sit with guilt without acting on it. Sometimes guilt only means I broke a dysfunctional family rule.
- Get honest; tell my truth and act on it.
- Let go of the drama produced.
- Set boundaries, even if it means walking away.
- Expect to be seen as the "bad guy" by those still on it.
- From *Detachment from an Addicted Relationship*: "get off your partner's back, get out of your partner's way, get on with your life." Noticing what someone is doing is fine; responding with opinions, suggestions or requests to change is where we step onto the triangle.
- From *Guidelines for Being in a Relationship*: don't assign blame, don't keep score, don't argue perceptions, don't threaten abandonment, and say what you need in four sentences or less. These guidelines are for you to follow, whether or not the other person does.
- ⚑ Review: both articles are written for partner relationships. Tell the room they apply to any relationship, and that you don't need to cover them all; pick the one or two that fit your story.

### Slide 17 — The Empowerment Dynamic (source)
- Section divider. Content is from *The Power of TED* by David Emerald.
- Say: "The triangle has an opposite. Same three seats, but everyone takes responsibility for themselves."
- ⚑ Review: TED stands for The Empowerment Dynamic, as on the slide; say the full name the first time.

### Slide 18 — What is TED?
- All roles take responsibility for themselves.
- Creator: focuses on what inspires them; outcome-focused and passion-powered; generates forward energy.
- Challenger: consciously builds others up; focuses on using the circumstances for growth and learning.
- Coach: sees the internal creator in others; above all remains supportive and encouraging.

### Slide 19 — Coach, Challenger, Creator
- Diagram of the three TED roles.
- Say: *"All roles value themselves while also looking for a way to value the other."*
- Pairing, for the exercise: Victim becomes Creator, Persecutor becomes Challenger, Rescuer becomes Coach.
- ⚑ Review: the pairing above is the standard TED mapping, not shown on these slides. Offer it as a helpful lens, not a rule.

### Slide 20 — Exercise: Find your starting gate (8 min)
- Run-sheet:
  1. Handouts out. Think of a recent conflict or tense moment (1 min). Choose something small, not your hardest relationship.
  2. On the handout, map it: who was the persecutor, the rescuer, the victim?
  3. Mark where you stepped onto the triangle. Which way in: denied feelings, dishonesty or failed intimacy?
  4. Write what a Creator, Coach or Challenger response would have looked like.
  5. 5 minutes writing, then 3 minutes sharing with one partner.
  6. Debrief (2 min): "Did anyone find that their starting gate surprised them?" Take one or two answers, no discussion.
- Remind people they can pass on sharing.
- ⚑ Review: the "which way in" step (3) and the debrief question are suggestions; the slide only lists the map, the Creator/Coach/Challenger question and the timing. The 1-minute thinking time comes out of the 5-minute writing block.

### Slide 21 — How do I fix unhealthy self-esteem?
- Start with Reality: learn what you are responsible for. Thoughts, feelings, choices, limits, behavior, body.
- Contain your reality with Boundaries: where do my responsibilities end and someone else's start? What limits do I want to place on my thoughts?
- Understand your wants and needs with Self Care: find out what they are, and which ones I want to share so others can help meet them.
- Set goals for moderation: give myself permission to miss the mark, then ask "How do I want to do it different next time?" Look at my reality and value moderate responses.
- Look for feelings of self-worth and value generated by the above.
- Validate what you have done. Celebrate the little wins along with the big ones. Trust the process.
- Say: "Self-esteem isn't something we fix by trying harder. It's what grows when the other four are in place."
- ⚑ Review: a quick way to teach this is as a recap of the whole class series, one line per class, pointing at your own Class 1 to 4 handouts.

### Slide 22 — Take-home: Where my value comes from
- Each day this week, notice one moment I looked to others for my worth (other-esteem), and one moment I found it in myself.
- Write them on the handout.
- At the end of the week: what do I notice?
- ⚑ Review: it counts even if the "in myself" moment is tiny. Also, this is the last teaching-hour take-home before the final class. Mention that next week we pull the whole series together.
