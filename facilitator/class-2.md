# Class 2 · Reality — Teaching Notes

**Teaching hour:** 60 min · **Group hour:** follows
**Big idea:** Reality is who we uniquely are: body, thoughts, feelings and behavior. The goal is to own it and share it without judgment, and to stop trying to control anyone else's.
**Bring:** Class 2 handout (the True / Not True / Questionable ladder), pens.
**Builds on:** Class 1 (Safe People & Feelings). Sharing our reality takes safe people, and we can only own our reality once we can name our feelings.

> **⚑ Review** marks anything added beyond the slides, the site articles and the old teaching notes.

## Head: What & Why (10 min)

### Slide 3 — What is it?
- Each of us has a unique way of interpreting life. Reality comes from within.
- It is shaped by our history and by how we respond to our environment.
- Two people can live through the same event. Each has different thoughts and feelings about it, and each decides on different actions.
- Say: *"Reality is how I experience life from the inside. Yours may differ, and neither of us is wrong."*
- ⚑ Review: a quick example to make it concrete. Two people at the same party: one found it energizing, one found it draining. Same event, two realities.

### Slide 4 — What makes up our Reality?
- Walk the list. From *Difficulty Owning Our Own Reality*, our reality has four core components:
  - **Body:** how we look and how our bodies function.
  - **Thoughts:** how we give meaning to sensory input.
  - **Feelings:** recognizing and experiencing our emotions.
  - **Behavior:** what we do and don't do, and how it impacts others.
- The slide also lists **Choices** and **Limits** under behavior.
- These are what make each person uniquely who they are, even when others would experience the same situation differently. (*Difficulty Owning Our Own Reality*)

## Hand: Motivation (8 min)

### Slide 5 — Can we own it?
- Difficulty owning your reality means struggling to experience who you are and share it with others.
- Signs on the slide:
  - We try to convince others of who we are, changing their reality about us.
  - We try to control what others think so we can meet someone else's expectations.
- Owning our reality is a gradient. We may not know our reality; we may know it but not be able to share it; or we may know it and share it regardless of what others think.
- Where we land depends on the situation and the people we are with.
- From *Difficulty Owning Our Own Reality*: codependents often report that they don't know who they are. In each part of reality the struggle looks different:
  - Body: hard to see our appearance accurately or notice how our body is operating.
  - Thinking: hard to know our thoughts, or to share them, or we give skewed interpretations to what comes in.
  - Feelings: hard to know what we feel, or feeling overwhelmed.
  - Behavior: hard to be aware of what we do, or to own it and its impact on others.
- From *Why We Are Afraid to Tell People Who We Are*, some reasons it is scary:
  - fear of rejection ("all we have is who we are")
  - family rules taught us to hide to survive
  - we don't trust our thoughts, feelings or opinions
  - we feel guilty about having needs and wants
- Say: "Nobody is all the way at one end. It shifts with who is in the room."
- ⚑ Review: ask the room to silently place themselves on the gradient with their family, then with a friend. No sharing needed.

### Slide 6 — What is the goal?
- Definition, word for word: *"A person who can share their reality with another person without judgment or a need to fix, change, or teach anything."* This is the healthy, mature adult.
- We get there by learning to **acknowledge, own, express and support** our reality.
- We also learn to accept others without judgment. (*Tools to Help You Own Your Own Reality*: the mature adult is one who can share reality with me and I with them.)
- Say: "The goal is not a perfect reality. It is a reality that is ours, shared without needing anyone to agree."

## Heart: Tools (32 min)

### Slide 7 — Tools for experiencing our Reality (1)
- **Be aware:** Are others trying to change your reality, or are you trying to change theirs? Did you let others share their thoughts? (*Tools to Help You Own Your Own Reality*: the exceptions are people we invite, like a therapist or sponsor.)
- **Claim it:** learn to surface, own, express and support your reality by becoming aware of your body, thoughts, feelings and behavior. (*Tools*: learn what our parents did not teach.)
- **Own it:** find where you are on the gradient, then strengthen your sense of reality by bringing it into awareness and expressing it to others.
- From *Tools*, if you know your reality but won't acknowledge it, practice acknowledging it to others when it's in your best interest, and accept compliments. If you don't know it, make a guess and let it go if it doesn't fit; your body will tell you if it's right. This may call for professional help.

### Slide 8 — Tools for experiencing our Reality (2)
- **Practice:** allow others to have their reality, even when it conflicts with yours about you.
- *Tools* adds: you can only do this with good boundaries. Boundaries give enough separation to resist owning someone else's reality about you that doesn't fit. ⚑ Review: this previews Boundaries, Class 3.
- Introduce **True / Not True / Questionable.** Pick a situation you are dealing with and ask: "Is it True, Not True, or Questionable that...?" A safe person can help give perspective.
- ⚑ Review: this is where Class 1's safe people come in. Name that connection out loud.

### Slide 9 — True, not true, or questionable: start extreme
- Ask: *"Is it True, Not True, or Questionable that...?"*
- Start with the most extreme thing your feelings are telling you. It's usually easy to call that one Not True.
- Step down one notch at a time until you reach the facts and your feelings.
- Watch for the ANTs: "never," "always," "I'm not good enough," "they think..."
- Then sort:
  - True: own it
  - Not True: let it go
  - Questionable: check it out with the person or a safe person, or wait
- ⚑ Review: ANTs = automatic negative thoughts, the site article *Automatic Negative Thoughts* covers them if people ask.

### Slide 10 — Example: "My friend didn't text me back all day"
- Walk the ladder down, one question at a time. Have the room call out True / Not True / Questionable before you reveal:
  - "...nobody will ever love me?" Not True
  - "...my friend hates me?" Not True: we had lunch last week and she hugged me goodbye
  - "...I did something to upset her?" Questionable: I don't know
  - "...she didn't text me back today?" True
  - "...I feel hurt and anxious?" True
- Land it: own the hurt, let go of "nobody loves me," check out whether she's upset.
- Say: "Notice we didn't argue the feeling away. The hurt stays. We just stop treating the extreme story as fact."

### Slide 11 — Exercise: Start extreme, then dial in (10 min)
- Run-sheet:
  1. (0 min, before timer) Handouts out. Read the instructions together.
  2. (5 min) Write down something someone said or did that stung. Start with the most extreme thing your feelings tell you, then step down with "Is it True, Not True, or Questionable that...?" until you reach the facts and your feelings.
  3. (included in the 5 min) Sort each answer: own what's true, let go of what's not, check out what's questionable.
  4. (3 min) Share with one partner. Courtesies apply: no fixing, no advice, pass is fine.
  5. (2 min) Debrief: "What was the most extreme thing you wrote? What happened to it as you stepped down?"
- Remind people they only share what they choose to.
- ⚑ Review: if someone is stuck, suggest a small sting, not their deepest wound. Total 10 min.

### Slide 12 — How We Try to Control Others (source)
- Section title. Material is again from *Facing Codependence*.
- Say: "So far we have owned our own reality. Now the flip side: where we try to control someone else's, or let them control ours."
- From *Negative and Positive Control*: our frustration and confusion come largely from trying to control other people's reality and from letting theirs control us.

### Slide 13 — Where does Negative Control come from? (1)
- Definition, from *Negative and Positive Control*: negative control is giving ourselves permission to decide for someone else how they should look, think, feel or act. Letting someone do that to us is also negative control.
- **Self esteem:** If you feel "better than," you have no trouble telling others what to look like, think, feel or do. If you feel "less than," you easily allow others to control you.
- **Reality:** If your reality is "good and perfect" (which nobody is, we are human), you have no trouble controlling others. If you have no idea who you are or feel "bad" (which nobody is, we are human), you easily allow others to control you.

### Slide 14 — Where does Negative Control come from? (2)
- **Boundaries:** If you have no boundaries, you have no trouble crashing someone else's, or allowing others to crash yours.
- **Self care:** If you are totally independent, you can easily try to control others because you don't feel you need anything from anybody. If you are totally dependent, you need others to provide your needs and wants.
- Point out the pattern: these are the other core symptoms. Negative control grows where they are out of balance.
- ⚑ Review: this is a preview of Classes 3 and 4. No need to teach them tonight.

### Slide 15 — Negative Control
- Words of negative control: *You should* (guilt trips), *You always*, *You never*, *You need*.
- From *Negative and Positive Control*, negative control can touch any part of reality: thoughts, feelings, choices, limits, behavior, body, desires, in both directions (telling others, or being told).
- Ask: "Which of these do you hear the most, from others or from your own mouth?" Rhetorical; no answers needed.

### Slide 16 — Take-home: Practice the ladder
- Once this week, when something stings, run the ladder.
  - Start with the most extreme thing your feelings are telling you.
  - Step down to the facts and your feelings.
  - Write it on your handout.
  - Own what's true, let go of what's not, check out what's questionable.
  - Notice how you feel afterward.
- Bring what you noticed to next week.
- ⚑ Review: from *Tools to Help You Own Your Own Reality*, if a part of your reality brings a "shame attack," remind yourself you have a right to your reality. Consider offering this gently here, and pointing to counseling resources if it feels heavy.
