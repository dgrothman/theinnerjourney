# Class 4 · Self Care — Teaching Notes

**Teaching hour:** 60 min · **Group hour:** follows
**Big idea:** Self care starts with knowing what we need and want, meeting what we can ourselves, and asking for help without shame. That is interdependence.
**Bring:** Class 4 handout (pattern exercise, asking-for-help exercise, wants experiment), pens.
**Builds on:** Class 3 (Boundaries). Boundaries contain us and start to deal with others and life events. Wants and needs become clear once we bring our Reality and Boundaries into relationships.

## Head: What & Why (12 min)

### Slide 4 — What are our needs and wants?
- Say: "Needs are the basic things required to survive. Wants are the preferences, items or goals we look to include in our life."
- Needs:
  - All people have dependency needs, children and adults alike. A child has to have them met by a caregiver and be taught how to meet each one. An adult is responsible for knowing how to address each need and for asking for help when help is truly needed. (*Difficulty Acknowledging and Meeting Our Own Needs and Wants*)
  - Adult needs: food, shelter, clothing, medical and dental attention, physical nurturing, emotional nurturing (time, attention and direction from others), sex, and financial resources (earning, saving, spending, budgeting, investing). (*Difficulty Acknowledging and Meeting Our Own Needs and Wants*; old notes)
  - Some needs, like physical or emotional nurturing, can only be met through interaction with another person.
- Wants:
  - Wants come from my Reality and are a choice. (old notes)
  - Big wants shape many life choices and our direction, such as "I want to be married to this person" or "I want to be a doctor." (*Difficulty Acknowledging and Meeting Our Own Needs and Wants*)
  - Little wants are preferences. We don't have to have them, but they bring us joy. (*Difficulty Acknowledging and Meeting Our Own Needs and Wants*)

### Slide 5 — Where wants and needs show up
- Say: "Wants and needs become apparent as we bring our Reality and Boundaries into relationships."
- Knowing our wants and needs involves understanding both our Reality and our Boundaries. (old notes)
- ⚑ Review: Tie it back. You can't know what you want if you don't yet know what you think and feel (Class 2), and you can't protect it without a fence (Class 3).

### Slide 6 — What is the issue?
- Say: "Codependents have difficulty meeting their own wants and needs. It can show up in a few different ways."
- Too dependent: I am aware of my needs and wants, but I expect other people to take care of them for me. I wait, expecting them to know. (slide; *Difficulty Acknowledging...*)
- Anti-dependent: I am aware of my needs and wants, but I am unwilling to accept help from anyone. I would rather go without than be vulnerable and ask. (slide; *Difficulty Acknowledging...*) Underneath is a fear of being hurt. (old notes)
- Wantless and needless: I am not aware of my needs and wants.
  - Old notes: this doesn't change until their Reality is built.
- Confusing needs and wants: I know what I want and I get it, but I don't know what I need. Example from the article: needing physical nurturing, and buying new clothes instead. (*Difficulty Acknowledging...*)
- Reassure the room: most of us are a mix. Tonight is noticing, not grading.

## Hand: Motivation (7 min)

### Slide 7 — Where these patterns come from
- Walk the four, one line each:
  - Too dependent: our parents took care of everything instead of teaching us to meet our own needs.
  - Anti-dependent: we were attacked for having and expressing needs and wants.
  - Needless and wantless: our needs and wants were ignored or neglected, so we never learned to name them.
  - Confusing needs and wants: we got everything we wanted but little of what we needed, like physical and emotional nurturing.
- ⚑ Review: Say this gently. This is about how the pattern formed, not about blaming parents. Remind people they can pass on sharing.

### Slide 8 — Why asking feels selfish
- Say: "When we feel a need or want, shame flares up."
- It started in childhood, when expressing a need or want was met with abuse or neglect. We may have forgotten the memory, but the feeling remains. (slide; *Difficulty Acknowledging...*)
- So we feel selfish for needing or wanting something, however legitimate it is.
- Recovery is interdependence: noticing our needs and wants, meeting what we can ourselves, and asking for help without shame or guilt.
- Motivation, word for word from the old notes: *"Knowing my wants and needs allows me to be responsible for what I want to allow."*
- We also learn to meet others' needs at appropriate times in proper circumstances. That is interdependence too. (*Difficulty Acknowledging...*)

## Heart: Tools (31 min)

### Slide 9 — Exercise: Which pattern is mine? (10 min)
- Run-sheet:
  1. (1 min) Handouts out. Mark the pattern(s) you recognize: too dependent, anti-dependent, needless and wantless, confusing needs and wants.
  2. (5 min) Write alone on two questions: "How were needs and wants handled in the home I grew up in?" and "Where do I see that pattern in my life now?"
  3. (3 min) Share with one partner. Remind people they can pass.
  4. (1 min) Debrief: "Did anyone notice more than one pattern?" Normalize it. Most people have a mix.

### Slide 10 — What does it look like?
- Say: "Sharing our Reality and Boundaries in a relationship lets us connect with the other person, which is an innate need we all have."
- Three ways that sharing can look: interdependent, anti-dependent, enmeshed. The next three slides show each one.

### Slide 14 — Skills for developing our wants and needs
- Work on your vulnerability (for anti-dependents):
  - Decide if the person is safe.
  - Decide which wants and needs you are willing to share with them.
  - Be willing to feel the fear when sharing what you choose to share.
  - Work on trusting yourself to handle what the other person does with your shared reality.
- Work on independence (for enmeshed):
  - Decide which wants and needs you are responsible for meeting.
  - Ask for help identifying what you can meet yourself.
  - Start taking steps to meet those wants and needs yourself.

### Slide 15 — More skills for developing our wants and needs
- Work on understanding your wants and needs by developing your Reality and Boundaries. That naturally develops your sense of what you want and need.
- Practice deciding what you want in low-pressure situations.
- Begin to express the wants and needs you are willing to share.
- Understand the other person may not be used to hearing them.
- From *Skills for Becoming an Adult*: make your own decisions; you are an adult and can learn to think and act for yourself. Give yourself permission to fail, because no one becomes an expert without trial and error.
- ⚑ Review: Low-pressure examples to offer: picking the restaurant, the movie, or what to have for dinner.

### Slide 16 — Exercise: Asking for help (8 min)
- Run-sheet:
  1. (2 min) Write down one need that takes another person to meet. Examples: time, attention, direction, or physical comfort.
  2. (1 min) Name one safe person you could ask.
  3. (2 min) Write the words you would use: *"Would you be willing to...?"*
  4. (2 min) Say it out loud to a partner. Partners just listen and say thank you.
  5. (1 min) Debrief: "What was it like to say it out loud?" Take one or two answers.
- Take-home: the wants experiment (see your handout). Bring what you noticed to next week.
- ⚑ Review: Remind people that asking is not a promise of a yes. Part of the practice is trusting ourselves to handle the answer (slide 14).
