# Class 6 · Moderation & Grace, Truth, Time — Teaching Notes

**Teaching hour:** 60 min · **Group hour:** follows
**Big idea:** Moderation is living in the middle of the extremes. We get there by accepting our real selves with grace, telling the truth about who we are, and giving the journey time.
**Bring:** Class 6 handout (ideal self / real self exercise, unsent letter), pens.
**Builds on:** Classes 2 to 5 (Reality, Boundaries, Self Care, Self Esteem). Moderation shows up inside each of the other four symptoms. This is the final class: close the six weeks.

> **⚑ Review** marks anything added beyond the slides, the site articles and the old teaching notes.

- Share briefly about a time you lived in an extreme: all or nothing, perfect or hopeless, overdoing it or checking out. Keep it under two minutes and model "sharing my reality, not fixing anyone."

## Class Intro (3 min)

### Slide 1 — The Inner Journey, Class 6
- Title: *Now let's add another piece of the puzzle: Moderation.*
- Say: "Five weeks ago we started this journey. Tonight we put the last piece in place, and then we look at how to keep walking."

### Slide 2 — Moderation (source)
- Material is from *Facing Codependence* by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller & J. Keith Miller. It is on the Resources page.

### Slide 3 — The five core symptoms
- Point to the full picture: Reality, Boundaries, Self Care, Moderation, Self Esteem. Each slide label is a piece: Reality, Needs & Wants, Moderation, Boundaries, Value.
- Say: "Moderation touches every one of the other four. It is both a symptom on its own and a part of each of the others."
- *Moderation* (article): it is sometimes called the either/or syndrome or the all-or-nothing approach to life.

## Head: What & Why (14 min)

### Slide 4 — What are sources of moderation issues?
- Two sources, from *Where Moderation Issues Come From*:
  - Observing and reacting to immoderate caregivers. Children model what they see. Some do the exact opposite of what mom and dad did, but because what they are reacting against was extreme, the "solution" is extreme too.
  - Feeling invisible. Childhood needs were ignored unless the child behaved in an extreme way to get attention. As adults we express ourselves in exaggerated ways, hoping to be heard and noticed.
- On the slide: needs were ignored, wants were the focus, extreme behaviors received attention.
- Repetition compulsion: a desire to fix or master something from our past, so we recreate it.
- Ask: "Do you reflexively react, or choose to respond?" Rhetorical; no answers needed.
- Value check: "Do we need to check it out?" Say: "If this sounds like your story, it is worth a closer look."

### Slide 5 — Are you feeling overwhelmed?
- Say: "If you are overwhelmed, check your moderation. Overwhelm is often a sign we are pushing in one extreme."
- Pause a beat. This is a quick practical hook for the whole evening.

### Slide 6 — What is Moderation?
- Balance: let the pendulum swing from one extreme to the other, to find the calm in the middle. We do not have to do more today than what is reasonable.
- Empathy: having strong internal boundaries. We can share an experience without sharing the feelings (taking them on).
- Acceptance: moments in time do NOT define us; we are NOT our behavior. A mistake is only a mistake. Avoid the shame bind. Welcoming vulnerability makes fear recede.
- Say: "Moderation is not boring or beige. It is the calm in the middle."

### Slide 7 — The either/or extremes
- Walk the table, one symptom at a time (from *Moderation*):
  - Self esteem: less than vs. better than
  - Boundaries: too vulnerable vs. invulnerable
  - Reality: rebellious/bad vs. perfect/good
  - Needs and wants: too dependent vs. antidependent (needless/wantless)
  - Moderation itself: immature/chaotic vs. overmature/controlling
- Say: "Moderation is living in the middle of each line."
- ⚑ Review: ask the room which row feels like their home turf. One or two answers, no discussion.

### Slide 8 — How does Moderation impact our Reality (body, thinking, behavior)
- From *Difficulty Experiencing and Expressing Our Reality Moderately*: not knowing how to be moderate is possibly the most visible symptom of codependence to other people.
- Body: how a person dresses or sees themselves.
- Thinking: black or white, right or wrong, good or bad. Few gray areas.
- Behavior: over the top or absent. Examples from the article: trusting everyone or no one; letting anyone touch us or no one at all; disciplining children severely or not at all.

### Slide 9 — How does Moderation impact our Reality (feelings)
- Per the article, the heart of the difficulty is knowing what our feelings are and how to share them. Many feel little or nothing, or have explosive or agonizing ones.
- Four kinds of feeling reality:
  - Adult feeling: a mature, authentic emotional response to our thinking. Usually moderate; we feel centered. It indicates Reality, Boundaries and Self Care are working in a healthy way.
  - Adult-induced feeling: the result of empathy. A healthy adult can feel a little of what someone shares without being swallowed by it.
  - Frozen feelings from childhood: feelings frozen at a moment in time, usually anger, pain or fear. We may respond in ways that are not age appropriate, or have a minimal response to a significant event.
  - Adult-to-child carried feelings: shame, rage, fear and pain absorbed from an adult and carried into adulthood. We feel overwhelmed and out of control (shame).
- Say: "Until we can tell these four apart, life can be very baffling and confusing."
- ⚑ Review: this is a lot of content. Slow down on the last two and keep the examples gentle. Remind the room that frozen and carried feelings are not a character flaw; they are what a child did to survive.

## Hand: Motivation (7 min)

### Slide 10 — Ideal Self versus Real Self (source)
- Material is from *Changes That Heal* by Dr. Henry Cloud. It is on the Resources page.
- Say: "This is where moderation gets personal."

### Slide 11 — Ideal Self versus Real Self
- We tend to solve the problem of good and bad by *separating* the two. That creates an inability to tolerate weakness and failure in ourselves or others.
- To get through life, we have to be able to live with both.
- The ideal self is imagined perfection: internalized values from our upbringing, coupled with our own wishes for ourselves.
- The real self is the one we truly are: weak, broken, and not what we imagined.

### Slide 12 — Relationships between them
- Problem: the ideal self judges the real self as BAD, so we deny part of who we are. This leads to secondary symptoms.
- Intrinsic solution: acceptance of the "bad." Ask, "What can I do better?" Value the real self as perfectly imperfect.
- Say: *"The path to peace is to call a truce with yourself."*
- ⚑ Review: ask for a quiet show of hands: "Who has a voice in their head that sounds like the ideal self?" No sharing. It sets up the exercise.

## Heart: Tools (30 min)

### Slide 13 — Exercise: My ideal self and my real self (12 min)
- Run-sheet:
  1. (1 min) Handouts out. Two columns: "ideal self" and "real self."
  2. (5 min) Fill in both columns alone across body, thoughts, feelings, behavior. Then answer the four questions:
     - How much of my real self do others know?
     - What messages does my ideal self send my real self?
     - Where do those messages come from?
     - How do I change those messages?
  3. (5 min) Share with one partner. Remind people they can pass on sharing.
  4. (1 min) Debrief: "What did you notice about the messages your ideal self sends?" Take one or two answers.
- ⚑ Review: the slide gives 5 minutes to write and 5 to share. The extra 2 minutes for set-up and debrief are a suggestion; trim the debrief if you are running behind.

### Slide 14 — Grace, Truth, and Time (source)
- Material is from *Changes That Heal* by Dr. Henry Cloud.
- Say: "Grace, truth and time are the three ingredients of growth. When they come together, the true self can finally be loved, accepted and grow." (*Grace, Truth & Time*)
- ⚑ Review: the site articles are written with faith language. Use or soften it to fit your room; the slides themselves are secular.

### Slide 15 — Grace to be stuck, truth to get out, time to trust the journey
- Say: "GRACE to be stuck. TRUTH to get out of it. TIME to trust the journey."
- Why we need all three, from *Good Time, Bad Time*:
  - Grace and time, without truth, make us comfortable in our stuckness.
  - Truth and time, without grace, bring discouragement and judgment.
  - Grace and truth, without time, give a vision without completion.
- *"They must go together."*

### Slide 16 — Grace
- Definition: undeserved kindness, as opposed to complacency.
- The positive: bestowing compassion and love on a person (or ourselves), and wanting nothing in return. From *Grace*: there is nothing you do to gain it; someone is on our side, for us instead of against us.
- *Grace* article: if getting help depends on our performance, that is a Catch-22. We need help to get better, and we cannot get the help until we are better.
- The negative: complacency. We deny the problem exists, and we cannot experience intimacy because there is no truth to connect with. Without truth we are free to behave as we wish, even if it is excessive or unacceptable.
- Listen for the lines on the slide: "That's the best I could do." "I can't help that he reacted that way."
- Ask: "Where do you get support that does not depend on how you are doing?" (*Grace*) Rhetorical.

### Slide 17 — Truth
- Truth is the structure of life: reality and its limits; society's direction and guidance; who we are and how that fits in. From *Truth*: the simplest way to think of truth is reality.
- The question to ask: "Do I need to clean it up?"
- Coming up short, and struggling with success, leads to painful feelings. Without grace we are quick to judge and "should" all over ourselves.
- *Grace, Truth & Time*: safe relationships are not just ones that give grace without confrontation. We need safe people who will be honest with us about where we need to change.
- *Truth*: there is the reality of who we are, and it needs to be present in our growth too. If we hide our reality, grace cannot reach it.

### Slide 18 — Time
- Say: "It's called a *journey* for a reason."
- Time is needed for change to occur. The pendulum swinging from one extreme to another settles with time. Messy is okay. Doing our best does not look the same every time.
- Let go of the fantasy of making a better past. It takes time to recognize grace and our need for it, and time for truth to sink in.
- From the old notes, word for word: *"I can't have a better past, and I can't control the future. I can only control the present."*
- *Good Time, Bad Time*: right now is the only place we can live. When we live in time, we are present with our experience, in the here and now, in our reality.
- *Grace, Truth & Time*: time is not a luxury but a necessity.

### Slide 19 — Forgiveness (source)
- Material is from *Forgiving Our Parents, Forgiving Ourselves* by Drs. David Stoop and James Masteller.
- ⚑ Review: this is the last tool of the six weeks. Slow down and keep the pace gentle; forgiveness can touch deep hurt. Remind the room that nobody has to forgive anyone tonight.

### Slide 20 — Two paths after a hurt
- An injury or hurt leads to a choice.
- Path of forgiveness: blaming stops, I cancel the debt, freedom and recovery.
- Path of bitterness: blaming continues, I nurse the blaming, bitterness and resentment.
- Link back to Class 3: "Resentment is always a boundary issue."

### Slide 21 — What forgiveness is (and isn't)
- Say, word for word: *"Forgiving a person who has hurt me means that I give up the need for revenge or punishment so that I can feel good inside myself. It doesn't mean that I must keep the person in my life… It doesn't mean I approve of the person's actions."* (*Facing Codependence*)
- Second quote: *"If we don't [forgive], we are handcuffing ourselves to the one who hurt us. Forgiveness is the knife that will cut through those handcuffs."* (*Changes That Heal*)

### Slide 22 — The six steps of forgiveness
- Walk the steps one line each:
  1. Recognize the injury: what happened, who did it, what effect did it have on me?
  2. Identify the emotions involved: fear, guilt and shame, anger.
  3. Express the feelings: talk them out with a safe person, or write them out.
  4. Set boundaries to protect yourself.
  5. Cancel the debt.
  6. Consider the possibility of reconciliation.
- Connect to earlier classes: step 3 is Reality (feelings), step 4 is Boundaries.
- ⚑ Review: "consider the possibility" is deliberate. Reconciliation is a question, not an expectation.

### Slide 23 — Forgiveness is not reconciliation; forgiving ourselves; take-home
- Forgiveness is something I can do on my own. It needs no cooperation from the other person, and they need not even know.
- Reconciliation takes two people who both own their part and work through it. It is not always possible.
- Forgiving ourselves: the IOUs we hold against ourselves are just as real and just as damaging.
- Take-home: the unsent letter (see your handout).
  - ⚑ Review: suggested directions. Write it to the person who hurt you, or to yourself. Say what happened and how it felt. Do not send it. Decide what to do with it afterward.
  - ⚑ Review: if the hurt involves abuse, suggest working through the letter with a counselor or a safe person.
- ⚑ Review: closing the six weeks. Name the arc: reality, boundaries, self care, self esteem, moderation. Say: "You do not have to do it all at once. This is a journey, and you have grace, truth and time." Thank the room.
