What is it?

Adapted from Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • Healthy self-esteem is the internal experience of one's own preciousness and value as a person
    • Comes from inside
    • Moves outward into relationships
    • Doesn't change based on behavior or surroundings

What is the issue?

Adapted from Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • People with complex trauma have difficulty with self-esteem at one or both extremes
    • Low self-esteem
      • I think I am worth less than others
      • I dont measure up
    • Arrogant
      • I think I am superior to others
      • I have learned to find faults in others, making them inferior to me
  • Both of these types of self-esteem result in what is called "Other-Esteem"
    • I get my value (sense of worth) from external things
      • It is based on my "human doing" or in the opinions and behavior of other people
      • Some examples
        • How I look
        • How much money I make
        • What I know
        • What kind of car I drive
        • What kind of job I have
        • How well my children behave
        • How attractive or powerful my spouse is
        • How well I perform

What does it look like?

Adapted from Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • Healthy Self-Esteem
    • Self-Esteem, Reality (Value)
      • I am enough
      • I Matter
      • I am perfectly imperfect
    • Boundaries, Reality, Moderation (Power)
      • I have enough control
      • I can influence my environment
      • I am in control of myself
    • Self-Care (Abundance)
      • I have enough
      • I can survive
      • I have joy in my life
  • Unhealthy Self-Esteem
    • Self-Esteem, Reality (Worthless)
      • Prestige
      • Other-esteem
      • I am never enough
    • Boundaries, Reality, Moderation (Helpless)
      • Power over others
      • Negative control
      • I never have enough control
    • Self-Care (Hopeless)
      • Money and possessions
      • Hoarding
      • I never have enough

How do I fix unhealthy self-esteem?

  • Start with Reality
    • Learn what you are responsible for: thoughts, feelings, choices, limits, and behavior
  • Contain your Reality with Boundaries
    • Where do my responsibilities end and someone else's start?
    • What limits do I want to place on my thoughts
  • Understand your wants and needs with Self Care
    • Find out what my wants and needs are
    • Find out what wants and needs I want to share with others to allow them to meet those needs
  • Set goals for moderation
    • Look at my Reality and value moderate responses
    • Give myself permission to miss the mark of moderation but ask myself "How do I want to do it different next time?"
  • Look for feelings of self-worth and value generated by the previous steps
    • Validate what you have done and celebrate the little wins along with the big ones
    • Trust the process that as you work on yourself, you will being to see yourself as more and more valuable

Bringing it together

Self esteem sits on top of everything we have worked on. Each week gave us a piece of it and a tool to practice. The readings, audio, and handouts for every class stay online at theinnerjourney.training.

Class 2 · Reality & Feelings

  • My reality has five areas: thinking, feeling, choices & limits, behavior, and body
    • Owning my reality gives me a sense of who I am
  • What I feel is what I feel; I can't change my feelings, but I can change my thoughts
  • Tools
    • True, Not True, or Questionable: separate the facts from my thoughts about them
    • Feel it, find the truth, talk it out with a safe person, release it in a way that harms no one
  • Remember: my reality is mine to own and to share

Class 3 · Boundaries

  • Boundaries are fences that protect my value
    • External boundaries protect my body; internal boundaries protect my reality
    • Resentment is always a boundary issue
  • A healthy boundary is a door with the knob on the inside: I choose when to open it
  • Tools
    • Saying "no" and setting limits
    • Putting boundaries into words: "I would like to revisit this later when the energy has dissipated"
  • Remember: I am responsible for my reality, not for someone else's

Class 4 · Wants and Needs (Self Care)

  • Needs keep us alive and well; wants are our preferences and goals
  • The patterns: too dependent, antidependent, needless and wantless, or confusing needs and wants
  • Recovery is interdependence: meet what I can myself, and ask for help without shame
  • Tools
    • Practice naming wants in low-pressure moments
    • Ask a safe person: "Would you be willing to…?"
  • Remember: having needs and wants is not selfish

Class 5 · Moderation and Grace, Truth & Time

  • Moderation is living in the middle instead of the either/or extremes
  • My ideal self is imagined perfection; my real self is who I am
    • The question is not "Am I good or bad?" but "What am I doing?"
  • Grace without truth is license; truth without grace is judgment; together they give love with limits
  • Tools
    • Choose to respond instead of reflexively react
    • Forgiveness, and giving growth time
  • Remember: I am perfectly imperfect
