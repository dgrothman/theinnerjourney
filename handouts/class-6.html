Self Esteem (Value)

Finding Peace Within

What is it?

From Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • Healthy self-esteem is the internal experience of one's own preciousness and value as a person
    • Comes from inside
    • Moves outward into relationships
    • Doesn't change based on behavior or surroundings

What is the issue?

From Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • Codependents have difficulty with self-esteem at one or both extremes
    • Low self-esteem
      • I think I am worth less than others
      • I dont measure up
    • Arrogant
      • I think I am superior to others
      • I have learned to find faults in others, making them inferior to me
  • Both of these types of self-esteem result in what is called "Other-Esteem"
    • I get my value (sense of worth) from external things
      • It is based on my "human doing" or in the opinions and behavior of other people
      • Some examples
        • How I look
        • How much money I make
        • What I know
        • What kind of car I drive
        • What kind of job I have
        • How well my children behave
        • How attractive or powerful my spouse is
        • How well I perform

What does it look like?

From Facing Codependence by Pia Mellody, Andrea Miller, & J. Keith Miller

  • Healthy Self-Esteem
    • Self-Esteem, Reality (Value)
      • I am enough
      • I Matter
      • I am perfectly imperfect
    • Boundaries, Reality, Moderation (Power)
      • I have enough control
      • I can influence my environment
      • I am in control of myself
    • Self-Care (Abundance)
      • I have enough
      • I can survive
      • I have joy in my life
  • Unhealthy Self-Esteem
    • Self-Esteem, Reality (Worthless)
      • Prestige
      • Other-esteem
      • I am never enough
    • Boundaries, Reality, Moderation (Helpless)
      • Power over others
      • Negative control
      • I never have enough control
    • Self-Care (Hopeless)
      • Money and possessions
      • Hoarding
      • I never have enough

Understanding the Drama Triangle

From The Three Faces of Victim by Lynne Forrest (edited by Imago Dei Ministries)

  • Three roles: Persecutor, Rescuer, and Victim (Stephen Karpman's "Drama Triangle")
    • Anytime we refuse to take responsibility for ourselves, we are opting to play victim
    • All three roles are faces of victim: they start from victim and end back at victim
    • A "shame machine": moving around the triangle keeps the shame messages going
  • One-up, one-down
    • Rescuer and Persecutor sit at the top and come across as "better than"
    • The Victim is one-down; resentment builds until they retaliate (and become the persecutor)
    • Rescuer and Persecutor are the two opposite extremes of victim
  • Starting gate position
    • We each have a most familiar role, learned in our family, that becomes part of our identity
    • We still rotate through all three roles, sometimes many times a day
    • Example: Mom threatens Junior (persecutor), Dad steps in to defend him (rescuer), Mom turns on Dad (now he is the victim), or Junior turns on Dad: "I don't need your help!"
  • Inside our heads
    • We play all three roles in our own self-talk
    • "I'm so lazy" (persecutor → victim), then "I would have finished if it weren't for…" (rescuer)

The Three Roles

  • Rescuer: the enabler, fixer, and mediator
    • A twisted version of the desire to nurture and protect
    • Comes from an unconscious need to feel important; taking care of others is how they connect and feel worth
    • Core belief: my needs don't count ("If I take care of others well enough, long enough, I'll get my turn")
    • Greatest fear: nobody will be there for them, so they make themselves indispensable
    • Fosters dependency, and needs someone to take care of
    • As victim: martyrdom and depression ("After all I've done for you…")
    • As persecutor: withdraws their care-taking
  • Persecutor: the blamer
    • A twisted version of the desire to protect and provide; tries to "reform" others through force
    • Shame-based anger covers feelings of worthlessness; acting "better than" covers feeling "less than"
    • Must be right: preaching, blaming, lecturing, interrogating, attacking, getting even
    • Core belief: the world is dangerous ("Get them before they get me!")
    • Greatest fear: powerlessness; denies their weaknesses and needs someone to blame
    • As victim: justifies getting even ("If it weren't for you, I wouldn't have had to…")
  • Victim: "I can't make it"
    • A twisted version of the innocent, vulnerable, needy child within us
    • Denies their problem-solving ability and their own power; sees themselves as the "unfixable problem"
    • Core belief and greatest fear: I can't make it on my own, so they look for someone more capable to carry them
    • Rescues from one-down ("You're the only one who can help me")
    • As persecutor: resents being dependent; sabotages help, plays "Yes, but…", uses guilt to hook a Rescuer

How we end up on the triangle

  • Denied feelings: keeping others from feeling bad, or not allowing ourselves our own feelings
  • Dishonesty: when we deny our feelings we can't know (or tell) our truth, so we keep hidden agendas
  • Failed intimacy: believing we are unlovable makes keeping our distance feel necessary
  • No one takes responsibility for themselves, and everyone ends up hurt and angry

How do I get off the drama triangle?

  • Become aware of the triangle and of my starting gate position
  • Take responsibility for myself (own my reality)
    • Allow myself to have my feelings
    • Allow others to have their feelings, without rescuing them
  • Sit with guilt without acting on it; sometimes guilt only means I broke a dysfunctional family rule ("Don't talk about it", "It's selfish to take care of yourself")
  • Get honest with myself and others; tell my truth and act on what it shows me
    • Rescuer: admit my investment in keeping others dependent, and start meeting my own needs
    • Persecutor: own my part and the ways I hurt others
    • Victim: start my own self-care and recognize my own problem-solving ability
  • Be willing to let go of the drama produced
    • Set boundaries, even if it means walking away
  • Expect to be seen as the "bad guy" (persecutor) by those still on the triangle; that doesn't make it so

Alternative to the Drama Triangle

From The Power of TED by David Emerald

The Empowerment Dynamic

  • Generates confidence
    • through internal interaction or external communication keeps the confidence messages going
  • Creator
    • A healthy version of the victim
    • focuses on what inspires them
    • outcome-focused and passion-powered
    • generates forward energy
  • Challenger
    • A healthy version of the persecutor
    • consciously builds others up
    • focuses on using the circumstances for growth and learning
  • Coach
    • A healthy version of the rescuer
    • sees the internal creator in others
    • above all remains supportive and encouraging

Exercise: Find your starting gate

Think of a recent conflict or tense moment, and map it onto the triangle.

  1. What happened?
  2. Who was the persecutor, the rescuer, and the victim?
  3. Where did I step onto the triangle?
  4. What would a Creator, Coach, or Challenger response have looked like?

How do I fix unhealthy self-esteem?

  • Start with Reality
    • Learn what you are responsible for
      • Thoughts
      • Feelings
      • Choices
      • Limits
      • Behavior
  • Contain your Reality with Boundaries
    • Where do my responsibilities end and someone else's start?
    • What limits do I want to place on my thoughts
  • Understand your wants and needs with Self Care
    • Find out what my wants and needs are
    • Find out what wants and needs I want to share with others to allow them to meet those needs
  • Set goals for moderation
    • Look at my Reality and value moderate responses
    • Give myself permission to miss the mark of moderation but ask myself "How do I want to do it different next time?"
  • Look for feelings of self-worth and value generated by the previous steps
    • Validate what you have done and celebrate the little wins along with the big ones
    • Trust the process that as you work on yourself, you will being to see yourself as more and more valuable
